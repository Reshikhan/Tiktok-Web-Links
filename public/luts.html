Free LUTs for Cinematic Color Grading

By Rashid Khan · Updated October 2026

LUTs (Look-Up Tables) are the fastest way to give your footage a cinematic color grade — but only if you use them correctly. Here's what LUTs actually are, how to install them, and how to download my free pack.

What is a LUT?

A LUT is a file containing a mathematical formula that remaps the colors in your video. Think of it as a precise, repeatable color preset: it takes every color in your footage and shifts it according to a fixed transformation. Filmmakers use LUTs to apply consistent, film-like looks across entire projects in seconds — but a LUT is a starting point, not a finished grade.

The same LUT looks completely different on different footage. A LUT designed for flat/log video will crush footage shot in a standard profile. That's why understanding the basics below matters more than collecting hundreds of presets.

How to install LUTs in CapCut

  1. Download the LUT pack above and extract the .cube files to your phone.
  2. In CapCut, select your clip and open Adjust → Filters (or the color grading panel, depending on version).
  3. Look for the custom/import option and select the .cube file from your downloads.
  4. Apply it, then immediately reduce the filter intensity to around 60%.
  5. Fine-tune with brightness, contrast, and saturation sliders after the LUT is applied.

How to install LUTs in Premiere Pro

  1. Copy your .cube files into Premiere's LUT folder (or browse to them directly from the Lumetri Color panel).
  2. With your clip selected, open Lumetri Color → Creative → Look and choose your LUT from the dropdown.
  3. Lower the Intensity slider to 50–70%.
  4. Do your basic correction (exposure, white balance) in the Basic Correction section before the creative LUT.

How to install LUTs in DaVinci Resolve (free)

  1. On the Color page, right-click in the LUTs browser panel and choose the folder containing your .cube files.
  2. Right-click a LUT and apply it to a node — ideally a separate node after your correction nodes.
  3. Reduce the node's Key Output Gain to dial back intensity.

Five rules for LUTs that actually look good

  • Correct before you stylize. Fix exposure and white balance first. A LUT can't rescue bad footage — it only exaggerates its problems.
  • Match the LUT to your footage. Log-designed LUTs on standard-profile footage produce crushed, ugly results.
  • Never leave it at 100%. 50–70% intensity almost always looks better. Skin tones are your truth-teller — if faces look orange, back off.
  • Learn the basic tools. Exposure, contrast, white balance, and saturation do most of the real work. The LUT is the final creative push.
  • Stay consistent. One look across a whole video beats a different dramatic grade on every clip.
