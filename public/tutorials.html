Video Editing Tutorials

By Rashid Khan · Updated October 2026

You don't need an expensive camera to make videos that look professional. These tutorials walk you through the complete mobile filmmaking workflow — shooting, editing, and color grading — using free or affordable tools.

1. Shooting basics: light, stability, and framing

Before you touch any editing app, your footage has to be good. Three fundamentals matter more than any gear:

  • Light: Shoot facing your light source. The hour after sunrise and the hour before sunset (golden hour) give you soft, flattering light for free. Avoid harsh midday sun directly overhead.
  • Stability: Hold your phone with two hands, tuck your elbows into your body, and move slowly. Shaky footage instantly looks amateur — a small tripod is the best first accessory you can buy.
  • Framing: Turn on grid lines and place your subject on the intersecting thirds. Leave headroom, watch your backgrounds, and keep the horizon level.

2. The Blackmagic Camera app: your free cinema camera

Your phone's default camera app makes every creative decision for you — and that's why phone footage looks like phone footage. The free Blackmagic Camera app (iOS and Android) gives you manual control over shutter angle, ISO, white balance, and focus, plus professional monitoring tools like focus peaking and zebra stripes.

The two settings that change everything: shoot at 24 frames per second for the cinematic look, and follow the 180-degree shutter rule (shutter speed roughly double your frame rate — 1/48 at 24fps) for natural motion blur. I've written a complete walkthrough in my Blackmagic Camera guide.

3. Color grading with LUTs

Color grading is where flat phone footage transforms into cinematic video. The workflow is simple but the order matters: correct first, stylize second. Fix exposure and white balance before any creative look touches your footage. Then apply a LUT as a starting point — never at 100% strength — and dial it back to 50–70% for a natural result.

My free LUT pack includes cinematic presets designed for mobile footage, along with installation guides for CapCut, Premiere Pro, and DaVinci Resolve.

4. Editing in CapCut: cuts, pacing, and transitions

CapCut (free) handles everything a beginner needs:

  • Cut ruthlessly. Remove every second that doesn't earn its place. Tight pacing keeps viewers watching.
  • Cut on motion. Placing cuts during movement hides them and keeps energy flowing.
  • Go easy on transitions. Hard cuts and subtle zooms look professional; flashy transition packs usually don't.
  • Sound design matters. Layer subtle whooshes under transitions and keep background music 10–15 dB below dialogue. Viewers forgive imperfect video — they don't forgive bad audio.

5. Exporting in maximum quality for TikTok and Instagram

Your edit can look perfect and still upload looking soft. To preserve quality:

  • Export at your timeline resolution (1080p minimum, 4K if your phone handles it).
  • Use the highest bitrate your app allows — 20 Mbps or higher for 1080p.
  • Match your project frame rate to your footage (usually 24 or 30fps).
  • In TikTok's upload settings, enable high-quality uploads. On Instagram, toggle on upload at highest quality in settings.
Common mistake: screen-recording your own edit or re-compressing through multiple apps. Every re-encode loses detail — export once, at full quality, straight from your editor.

Keep practicing

The gap between beginner and intermediate isn't gear — it's reps. Shoot one short video every week applying a single technique from these tutorials, compare it against the previous week, and you'll see the progress compounding. Follow me on TikTok for new tutorials as I post them.
